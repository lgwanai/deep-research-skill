# Industry Expert Research

Use this reference when the user asks for industry analysis, market research, competitive intelligence, investment diligence, business strategy, sector outlook, category entry, or "expert-level" research.

The goal is not to sound like an expert. The goal is to reason like one: define the market precisely, inspect the value chain, quantify economics, identify constraints, test competing explanations, and produce decision-useful implications.

For domain-specific work, first read `references/expert-router.md` and load the matching expert file. This file remains the general industry research frame.

## When To Use

Use this template for:

- Industry reports and market maps
- Sector trend or outlook research
- Competitive landscape and strategic group analysis
- Investment, M&A, or due diligence pre-research
- Go-to-market and category entry research
- Supply chain, regulation, or business model analysis
- Expert interview preparation or simulated expert briefing

Do not use this template for simple product feature lookup or one-off factual questions.

## Expert Research Contract

Start by defining:

```text
Industry / category:
Geography:
Time window:
Decision context:
End customer / buyer:
Value-chain scope:
Key competitors / substitutes:
Output type:
```

If the user's scope is vague, ask one boundary question. If the answer can proceed with assumptions, state them.

## Expert-Level Research Map

For Pro/Ultra industry work, cover the dimensions below unless clearly irrelevant.

| Dimension | Core question | Preferred source types |
|---|---|---|
| Market definition | What is included/excluded? How is the category segmented? | industry associations, statistical agencies, analyst definitions, company filings |
| Market size and growth | How large is it, how fast is it growing, and under which definition? | official statistics, filings, analyst reports, trade bodies |
| Demand drivers | Why do customers buy? What triggers adoption? | customer surveys, case studies, transaction data, channel reports |
| Value chain | Who captures value upstream/midstream/downstream? | company filings, trade associations, supply-chain reports |
| Unit economics | What drives revenue, margin, capex, working capital, CAC, churn, utilization? | filings, investor decks, earnings calls, benchmarks |
| Competitive landscape | Who competes, on what basis, and where are white spaces? | company reports, market share data, product docs, third-party reviews |
| Regulation and policy | What rules, subsidies, approvals, standards, or compliance risks matter? | government, regulators, standards bodies, legal databases |
| Technology and operations | What technology, infrastructure, or operational constraints shape the market? | standards, patents, technical docs, engineering blogs |
| Distribution and channels | How does the product reach customers? Who has channel power? | channel reports, partner pages, marketplace data, trade media |
| Risks and inflections | What could change the market structure or invalidate the thesis? | risk factors, litigation, policy updates, security reports, critical commentary |

## Expert Interview Lens

When simulating an expert viewpoint or preparing interview questions, structure around:

1. Market boundary: "How do insiders define this category?"
2. Demand: "What buyer pain is real enough to pay for?"
3. Adoption: "What blocks conversion from pilot to scale?"
4. Economics: "Where are margins created or destroyed?"
5. Competition: "What do incumbents know that entrants underestimate?"
6. Regulation: "What must be true legally or operationally?"
7. Timing: "Why now, and what changed recently?"
8. Non-obvious risks: "What looks good in reports but fails in the field?"

For each question, seek evidence before presenting an answer. Label any simulated expert view as synthesis, not a real quote.

## Analysis Frameworks

Choose 2-4 frameworks that match the task. Do not stack frameworks for decoration.

| Framework | Use when | Output artifact |
|---|---|---|
| Value chain analysis | Industry structure and profit pools matter | upstream/midstream/downstream map |
| Porter's Five Forces | Competitive intensity and margins matter | force-by-force assessment |
| PESTEL | Macro, policy, or regulation matters | policy/economic/social/tech risk map |
| TAM/SAM/SOM | Opportunity sizing matters | market sizing with assumptions |
| Unit economics | Business model viability matters | revenue/cost/margin driver table |
| Strategic group map | Competitors cluster by model or positioning | 2-axis positioning map |
| Adoption curve | New technology or category maturity matters | maturity and adoption barriers |
| Scenario analysis | Future is uncertain and decision depends on assumptions | base/upside/downside cases |

## Quality Bar

An expert-grade industry answer should not stop at market size. It should include:

- A clear market boundary
- At least one primary or authority source
- A value-chain or competitor structure view
- A demand/adoption explanation
- A regulatory or constraint check when relevant
- Explicit economics or business model drivers
- At least one risk or disconfirming signal
- A confidence statement tied to evidence quality

## Common Mistakes

- Treating market size as the whole analysis
- Mixing global and local numbers without scope notes
- Comparing companies that operate at different value-chain layers
- Using vendor white papers as neutral evidence
- Ignoring regulation, standards, approvals, and procurement cycles
- Presenting "expert view" as if it came from a real interview
- Using a framework name without doing the analysis it requires
